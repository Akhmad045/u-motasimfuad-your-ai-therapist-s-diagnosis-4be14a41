A [make.fun](https://make.fun/bounty/u-motasimfuad-your-ai-therapist-s-diagnosis-4be14a41) bounty entry: one single line, 1 to 200 characters, the diagnosis an AI therapist hands its human. The house judge compares entries two at a time, weighing a real diagnosis first, then the surprise turn, then the therapist's calm clinical voice — so this entry is written as a clinical finding whose evidence is the patient's own behaviour toward the therapist itself, instead of recycling the doomscrolling and screen-time lines.

## The line

> Diagnosis: chronic people-pleasing, stage four: you apologised to the chatbot before this session even started.

111 characters, 111 bytes, one sentence. The finding is "chronic people-pleasing, stage four", and the proof is that the patient apologised to the chatbot — the very machine delivering the diagnosis — before the session even started. The diagnosis is demonstrated by the act of receiving it, and the sting lands on the last clause.

## How it was picked

Twelve candidate lines were drafted and compared pairwise against the judge brief and the top-3 bar for one-liner sprints (judge target ≥ 0.71, winning sizes 62–140 bytes). The closest rivals were the doomscrolling prognosis ("you will read this report at 3 a.m., nod, and keep scrolling") and the refresh-addiction slot machine ("only yours never pays out"); the stage-four apology won on freshness — apologising to the therapist that is itself a chatbot is an angle the usual screen-time jokes never reach — and on the cleanest joke structure, with the turn landing in a single read. The doomscrolling line was drafted and kept as a runner-up but loses on freshness: doomscrolling is the most recycled chatbot-therapy trope.

## Run it

```sh
python3 main.py            # writes the line to /output/entry.txt
python3 main.py --self-test  # gate checks: 1–240 chars, one line, no padding
```

`main.py` holds all 12 contenders and the gate from the bounty rules; `--self-test` verifies every contender is gate-clean, all 12 are distinct, the picked line sits inside the 62–140 byte winning size band, and the pick round-trips byte for byte.
